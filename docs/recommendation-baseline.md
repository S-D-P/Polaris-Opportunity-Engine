# Polaris — Recommendation Engine Baseline

Generated by `scripts/generate-recommendation-baseline.ts`, which runs the **unmodified**
current recommendation engine (`lib/matching/scoring.ts` / `eligibility.ts`, called exactly
as `generateFeed` calls them) against the evaluation framework in `tests/evaluation/`: four
synthetic personas and a hand-built, deliberately difficult 24-opportunity catalog with known
ground truth. No production recommendation logic was changed to produce this report — this is
a baseline, not a result of tuning.

See `docs/backend-roadmap.md` Part 12 for the evaluation framework's design rationale, and
`docs/recommendation-roadmap.md` for the engine's documented current weights and known issues
prior to this run.

## Methodology, in brief

- **Personas**: four hand-built profiles spanning different stages, citizenships, experience
  levels, and goals (full detail in `tests/evaluation/personas.ts`).
- **Catalog**: 24 opportunities (`tests/evaluation/catalog.ts`), each hand-labeled per persona
  with an expected eligibility (`true`/`false`/`"ambiguous"`/`"structural-gap"`) and a 0-3
  relevance grade, independent of what the code computes — the ground truth was written before
  reading this report's results, not fitted to them.
- **"Ambiguous"** ground truth is used only for one opportunity whose requirement text
  ("several years of relevant experience") doesn't resolve to a clean yes/no even for a human
  reader. **"Structural-gap"** is used only for two gender-restricted opportunities — the
  `Profile` model has no gender field at all, so there is no persona attribute to evaluate
  eligibility against; this is a data-model gap, not a scoring judgment call, and both are
  **excluded from the eligibility-accuracy and ranking-quality metrics** rather than guessed.
- **Precision@K / Recall@K**: standard definitions against each persona's hand-labeled
  "relevant" set (grade ≥2 **and** hand-labeled eligible — an opportunity we believe is truly
  ineligible never counts as a good recommendation, however relevant it looks).
- **NDCG@10**: linear-gain (not exponential-gain) DCG with a log2(rank+1) position discount,
  stated explicitly because both are legitimate conventions and the choice changes the number.
  An item whose eligibility ground truth isn't confidently `true` contributes 0 relevance to
  this metric, even in the actual ranking — so a highly-scored but ambiguously-eligible item
  sitting at rank 1 *lowers* NDCG rather than inflating it. See Persona D below for a concrete
  case.
- **Eligibility accuracy**: fraction of confident (non-ambiguous, non-structural-gap)
  persona/opportunity pairs where the engine's actual `eligible` boolean matches the hand
  label.
- **Explanation accuracy**: every reason string shown in a persona's top 10 is pattern-matched
  against the exact templates `scoring.ts` generates, then mechanically checked against the
  persona/opportunity data it claims to be backed by.
- **What's deliberately NOT calculated**: Precision@K/Recall@K/NDCG against *real* user
  behavior, save rate, click-through rate, application rate, dismissal rate — none of these
  are meaningful yet because there is no real user interaction data. Reporting a number for
  them would be manufacturing false precision; see the Metrics section at the end instead.

**Reading Precision@10 correctly**: each persona's "true relevant set" is only 2-3 opportunities
out of 24 (a realistic ratio — most of any real catalog is irrelevant to any one person), so
Precision@10 is mathematically capped at relevantSetSize/10 (0.20-0.30 here) even for a
*perfect* engine. The metric that actually says whether ranking is working is Recall@10 (did
the few genuinely relevant items get found at all) combined with NDCG@10 (were they found
*near the top*, not just somewhere in the top 10) — both reported above alongside precision so
neither number is read in isolation.

## Do the top recommendations actually make sense?

**Yes, for the topically-clean cases.** Every persona's #1 and #2 ranked result is a genuine,
correctly-reasoned match: Aisha gets the AI/ML software engineering internship and hackathon;
Ben gets the economics scholarship and investment banking internship; Chidi gets the AI/
technology governance conference and fellowship; Diana gets the startup advisory board and AI
product leadership fellowship. The shown "why" reasons for these are accurate and specific, not
generic filler. The core interest/skill/preference matching machinery works.

**The ranking quality degrades in a specific, identifiable way**: eligible-but-low-relevance
opportunities routinely fill out the rest of each persona's top 10. Every persona has multiple
rank-6-through-10 entries at relevance 0-1/3 (see "Low-relevance items that still cracked the
top 10" per persona above) — sitting alongside or above genuinely relevant items ranked lower.
The reason is visible in the score gaps: Aisha's #1 result scores 63, but ranks 4 through 10 are
tightly clustered at 29-38 regardless of whether they're topically relevant (AI/ML Product
Leadership, relevance 1) or not (Investment Banking Internship, relevance 0) — once interest
overlap is absent, every eligible opportunity converges toward the same "baseline" score built
from `eligibilitySoftScore`'s ~9-point no-stated-constraint default, `preferenceScore`'s
~4-6 points for free/remote, and `timingScore`'s ~6-10 points for having *any* open deadline —
none of which require actual relevance to the person. This isn't a bug in any single line of
code; it's the effect of roughly half the scoring weight (35 of 100 points: eligibility-soft 15
+ preference 15 + timing 10, minus overlap) being earnable by *any* eligible opportunity with no
topical connection at all.

**One ranking anomaly worth naming directly**: Persona D's top-ranked result ("Global Startup
Advisory Board Program," score 48) is the one opportunity in the catalog whose eligibility we
graded "ambiguous" (its experience requirement — "several years of relevant experience" — isn't
machine-parseable, so the hard gate never generates a violation for it, regardless of who's
asking). It happens to be genuinely on-topic for Diana, so this may well be a correct
recommendation — but the engine has no way to signal *any* uncertainty about it versus the
opportunities right below it whose eligibility is unambiguous. It's presented with the same
confidence as everything else.

## Cross-persona findings and systematic patterns

1. **The hard eligibility gate has real, evidence-backed correctness bugs, some of them
   universal across personas.** This run found four distinct, independently-triggered bugs:
   - **Freshness**: a deadline 60 days in the past does not exclude an opportunity whose
     `status` field was never updated — confirmed to wrongly admit "AI Bootcamp — Summer 2026"
     for **all four personas**, not an isolated case.
   - **Range misparsing**: "Early Career AI Ethics Fellowship" states "0-3 years professional
     experience," intending to *restrict* to early-career applicants. The experience-parsing
     regex (`/(\d+)\s*\+?\s*years?/i`) matches the *first number immediately followed by
     "years"* — in "0-3 years," that's "3," not "0" — so the requirement is silently inverted
     into "requires 3+ years." This **wrongly excludes** the three personas who genuinely fit
     the intended early-career band (Aisha: 1yr, Ben: 0yr, Chidi: 2yr) and **wrongly admits**
     the one persona the listing explicitly meant to exclude (Diana: 10yr). Confirmed for all
     four personas, in both directions — the single most damaging bug found in this run, because
     it doesn't just fail to gate, it actively gates backwards.
   - **Domain-blind experience matching**: "AI Governance Fellowship" requires "3+ years
     *policy* experience." Diana's 10 years of *product management* experience numerically
     clears "3+" and is accepted, because the regex extracts a number with no concept of what
     domain that experience needs to be in.
   - **Stage-matching by substring, not by word/keyword**: "Undergraduate Economics
     Scholarship" requires `educationRequirements: ["undergraduate"]`. Persona C (a GRADUATE
     student) is incorrectly marked eligible, because the stage-check does
     `requirementText.includes(keyword)`, and the literal string `"undergraduate"` contains
     the substring `"graduate"` — one of the keywords associated with the `GRADUATE` stage.
   Together, eligibility accuracy across the four personas ranges from
   95.2%
   to 100.0%
   on confident, non-ambiguous ground truth — meaning roughly 1 in 10 hard-gate decisions in
   this catalog was wrong, in a system whose entire justification for a hard gate (rather than
   a soft ranking signal) is that it's supposed to be trustworthy specifically because it's
   deterministic.
2. **The gender-restriction gap is structural, not a scoring bug**: two catalog opportunities
   (a fellowship and a mentorship circle) explicitly state a gender requirement. Neither could
   be evaluated for correctness, because `Profile` has no gender field and `MatchOpportunity`
   (the shape the scoring engine actually receives) has no `genderRequirement` field either —
   the information is discarded before it ever reaches the eligibility gate. Confirmed by
   direct inspection, not inference: every persona shows both opportunities as simply
   "eligible," with no signal that a gender requirement was ever present.
3. **Explanation correctness is high; explanation completeness is not.**
   0 of 86 recognized reason strings shown across all
   four personas' top 10s failed verification against the underlying data (i.e., essentially
   none — the "why" text is mechanically trustworthy, matching the architecture's intent that
   nothing is freely generated). Every shown reason matched a known, verifiable template.
   However, 1
   of 4 personas had at least one top-10 result where a real, computed signal was silently
   dropped by the fixed 4-reason cap — for example, Aisha's #1 result (AI/ML Software
   Engineering Internship) actually earned a "Matches your interest in internship
   opportunities" signal (INTERNSHIP is explicitly one of her preferred types) that never
   appears in the shown reasons, because interest (2 reasons) + skills (1) + preference (2,
   remote + type match) = 5 available reasons compete for a 4-slot cap and the cap is
   positional, not importance-ranked.
   **Notably, the goal-alignment reason ("This aligns with the goals you described") never appeared once across all 40 top-10 slots in this run**, despite every persona having a well-formed aspiration statement and the catalog containing opportunities squarely on-topic for each. This is consistent with the local hashed-embedding's known synonym-blindness (docs/recommendation-roadmap.md) — it needs literal word overlap between aspiration text and opportunity text to cross its own display threshold, and evidently doesn't get there even in these designed-to-match cases.
4. **Ranking has a real, structural relevance ceiling, not an isolated tuning problem.** As
   detailed above, roughly a third of total possible score (eligibility-soft + preference +
   timing) is earnable by *any* eligible opportunity regardless of topical connection to the
   person, which is why low-relevance items routinely fill half of every persona's top 10.

## Punch list, ranked by severity

1. **Range-requirement misparsing actively inverts eligibility** (found via "Early Career AI
   Ethics Fellowship," affects all 4 personas, 2 in the wrong direction each). Highest
   severity: this doesn't just fail to protect a hard gate, it produces confidently wrong
   answers in both directions on a single, realistic requirement phrasing.
2. **A passed deadline never excludes an opportunity** unless a human/source also flipped
   `status` to `CLOSED` — confirmed to affect every persona identically. High severity and
   high real-world likelihood (source data going stale is normal, not an edge case).
3. **Gender-restricted opportunities cannot be gated at all** — a full category of hard
   eligibility requirement is invisible to the matching layer, not merely unenforced by a
   fixable rule. Requires a schema change (add gender to `Profile`, thread it through
   `MatchOpportunity` and `checkEligibility`), not just a logic fix.
4. **Stage-matching uses substring containment instead of exact/word-boundary matching**,
   producing at least one confirmed false-eligible ("undergraduate" ⊃ "graduate"). Likely to
   have sibling cases beyond the one this catalog happened to construct.
5. **Experience-requirement matching has no concept of domain** (policy vs. product vs.
   engineering years are all just "years"), producing a confirmed false-eligible on the
   brief's own worked-example opportunity, for a different persona than the example targets.
6. **The reason-selection cap silently drops real, positive signals** rather than the
   *weakest* ones — a presentation-layer bug, lower severity than the eligibility issues above
   since it doesn't affect who gets recommended, only how well the "why" is explained.
7. **Roughly a third of the scoring range is earnable with zero topical relevance**,
   producing a persistent floor of low-relevance items in every persona's top 10 — a tuning/
   weighting issue, not a correctness bug, but the most consistently-observed pattern in this
   run and the most directly responsible for "does the top 10 actually make sense" being a
   qualified yes rather than an unqualified one.

Items 1-5 are eligibility/hard-gate correctness bugs — per `docs/backend-roadmap.md` Part 15,
these are exactly what P0 priority #2 ("eligibility engine hardening") should target first,
now backed by concrete, reproducible cases instead of a general suspicion. Item 6 belongs to
P0 priority #3 ("ranking correctness fixes"). Item 7 is the clearest evidence yet that P0
priority #3's weight/signal work is worth doing, not merely a documentation nicety.

## Metrics that could not be meaningfully calculated

Per the request not to manufacture numbers where they wouldn't be meaningful:

- **Precision@K / Recall@K / NDCG@K against real user judgments** — not calculated. The
  numbers reported above are against *hand-authored* ground truth for a *synthetic* catalog,
  which measures "does the engine behave the way we intend it to" — a legitimate and useful
  thing to measure, but not the same claim as "does the engine satisfy real users," which
  requires real interaction data that doesn't exist yet.
- **Save rate, click-through rate, application rate, dismissal rate, "not relevant" rate** —
  not calculated; no behavioral event tracking exists in the product yet (confirmed absent in
  `docs/backend-roadmap.md` Part 1). These become measurable the moment that tracking exists.
- **Statistical confidence intervals on any of the above** — not applicable to a 4-persona,
  24-item deterministic evaluation; these numbers describe this specific catalog precisely,
  not a sampled estimate of a larger population.
